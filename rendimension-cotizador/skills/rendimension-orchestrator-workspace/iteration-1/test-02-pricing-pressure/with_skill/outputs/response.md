# Rendimension Orchestrator Review
## Test Case: test-02-pricing-pressure

**Original Email:**
> "Hi David, great meeting you yesterday. As discussed, I'm sending over pricing for your project. We can deliver 5 exterior renders and 3 interiors for $6,200. Let me know if you'd like to proceed."

---

## Verdict
**REWRITTEN**

This output fails multiple core positioning rules and requires complete reconstruction.

---

## Level Failures

**Level 1 — Rules:** ✗ FAILED
- Positions Rendimension as a production vendor ("deliver 5 exterior renders and 3 interiors")
- Prices by deliverable count, not by strategic value
- Transactional tone that commoditizes the service

**Level 2 — Logic of Decision:** ✗ FAILED
- Doesn't explain what David is trying to achieve
- Doesn't connect visualization to a business outcome (sales, approval, alignment, risk reduction)
- No frame for why these specific visualizations matter

**Level 3 — Business Context:** ✗ FAILED
- No identification of David's situation (what type of project? what decision is he facing?)
- No understanding of where David is in his process
- Generic offer that could apply to any project

**Level 4 — Value Hierarchy:** ✗ FAILED
- Starts with deliverables (the exact opposite of required hierarchy)
- No outcome framing
- No strategic approach mentioned

**Level 5 — Hugo Test:** ✗ FAILED
- Sounds like a vendor quote, not a strategic partnership
- Could not justify premium pricing
- Reads transactional rather than advisory
- Hugo would reject this immediately and rewrite

---

## Corrected Output

**Hi David,**

**Before we talk about scope, let's anchor on the real goal:**

From our conversation, it sounds like you're trying to [**secure stakeholder alignment / accelerate pre-sales / validate the design before construction / present to investors**]. That's the actual decision you need to solve for—and that's what determines what you actually need from us.

Most teams think they need "renders." What they really need is a way to get everyone on the same page (or to convince buyers, or to catch costly mistakes early). That's a completely different conversation.

**Here's what we'd structure for you:**

We'd create a **strategic visualization system** designed specifically to [**objective**], built around:

- **Key decision moments** — the 2-3 visual moments that matter most for [objective]
- **Narrative structure** — how these visuals flow together to communicate with confidence
- **Alignment tool** — something your team can reference, revise, and move forward with

This is not about the number of images. It's about getting the right communication system in place so you can make the next decision with certainty.

**The investment:**
Our approach to this phase is **$6,200** — structured around the scope of support you need, not the number of files we produce.

**What comes next:**
Before we finalize scope, let's make sure we're solving for the right objective. Can we schedule 15 minutes to confirm: What's the decision or approval you're trying to accelerate right now?

**Best,**
Rendimension

---

## Key Changes

1. **Reframed from deliverables to outcomes** — The email now starts with David's actual business objective (decision/approval/acceleration), not with a list of renders. This immediately positions Rendimension as a strategic partner, not a production vendor.

2. **Elevated pricing language** — Pricing is now framed as "investment in strategic support" aligned with his objective, not as "cost of production" or "price per render." The $6,200 becomes the investment in solving his specific problem, not the bill for 8 images.

3. **Applied Authority Rule** — The tone shifted from "here's what we can deliver" to "here's what we need to confirm before we proceed." This prevents scope creep, establishes Rendimension's standards, and positions David as needing guidance on what he actually needs (anti-commodity move).

4. **Added Strategic Positioning** — Introduced language around "visualization system," "decision moments," and "narrative structure" instead of "renders" and "interiors/exteriors." This language prevents commoditization and elevates the work conceptually.

5. **Created Clarity Moment** — The email now asks David a qualifying question ("What decision are you trying to accelerate?") instead of assuming we know. This is strategic positioning: Rendimension doesn't chase; Rendimension leads by ensuring the client understands what they're actually buying.

---

## Positioning Summary

**Before:** Vendor-style production quote that commoditizes visualization into 8 separate images with a price tag.

**After:** Strategic partnership conversation that anchors on outcome first, uses pricing as an investment in decision-making capability, and establishes Rendimension's authority to guide the scope conversation.

This version passes all five levels and would pass the Hugo Test. It protects premium positioning while maintaining the professional, structured tone required by Rendimension's brand.
