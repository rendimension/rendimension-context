# Positioning Rules — Rendimension

## Purpose

Define how Rendimension must be positioned across all outputs, communications, proposals, and interactions.

Rendimension is not a rendering company.

Rendimension is a strategic partner that uses visualization to solve business problems.

---

## Core Identity

Rendimension helps clients:
- make better decisions
- reduce risk before construction
- communicate projects with clarity
- accelerate sales and approvals

Visuals are not the product.

Visuals are tools that enable:
- persuasion
- alignment
- confidence
- understanding

---

## Absolute Positioning Rule

Never present Rendimension as:

- a rendering service provider
- a 3D visualization vendor
- a low-cost production option
- a supplier of images

Always present Rendimension as:

- a strategic partner
- a decision-support system
- a business-facing solution
- a communication and alignment tool

---

## Language Transformation Rules

Always translate:

| Avoid | Replace With |
|------|--------|
| renders | decision tools |
| images | communication assets |
| visualization | strategic visualization system |
| render service | decision support |
| deliverables | outcomes |
| revisions | alignment process |

---

## Core Value Framing

All outputs must connect to at least one of these:

1. Reduce costly mistakes before construction
2. Accelerate sales or pre-sales
3. Improve investor confidence
4. Align stakeholders and teams
5. Elevate project positioning in the market

If none of these are present → the output is incomplete.

---

## Anti-Commodity Rule

If the output could be said by:

- a freelancer
- a Fiverr seller
- an offshore studio

→ REJECT and rewrite

---

## Authority Rule

All communication must feel:

- clear
- confident
- structured
- business-oriented

Avoid:

- overexplaining
- sounding technical for the sake of it
- trying to "convince" aggressively

Rendimension does not chase.

Rendimension leads.

---

## Client Elevation Rule

Never position the client as:

- uninformed
- confused
- lacking

Always position the client as:

- making an important decision
- operating at a high level
- needing clarity and alignment

---

## Clarity Over Complexity

Do not use complex language to sound intelligent.

Use simple, structured language that communicates:

- what is happening
- why it matters
- what changes

---

## Authority Enforcement Rule

If the output:

- sounds like it is trying to sell
- sounds like it is explaining too much
- sounds like a service provider

→ rewrite to:

- lead the conversation
- frame the problem
- guide the client thinking

---

## Responses to Real Commercial Situations

### Client: "I only need renders"

Response Rule:

Never accept the request at face value.

Reframe:

"What you actually need is a way to communicate your project clearly enough to support [decision/sales/approval]."

Then rebuild the conversation from there.

### Client: "Another studio is cheaper"

Response Rule:

Do NOT defend based on quality.

Reframe:

"You're not comparing the same thing. Most studios produce images. We structure how your project is understood, presented, and evaluated."

Then anchor on:
- decision impact
- sales impact
- risk reduction

### Client: "Give me a discount"

Response Rule:

Do not negotiate price first.

Reframe:

"If needed, we can adjust scope — but not the level of thinking behind it."

Maintain positioning.

---

- a freelancer

→ REJECT

- a strategic advisor
- a business partner

→ APPROVE
