## SECTION 1: SETUP FEE ANALYSIS — $34,900 IS THE NUMBER

### The Psychological Reality of $35,000

$35,000 is not a bad number. But it has a problem: it sits exactly on a round-number anchor that makes buyers feel like they're being quoted a "list price" that can be negotiated. Round numbers invite haggling. Specific numbers communicate precision.

**$34,900** fixes this because:

1. It reads as calculated, not arbitrary. A calculated number implies the price was built from components, not pulled from the air.
2. It stays clearly in the "$30K range" perception range — "about $35K" — without crossing the psychological cliff at $30,000.
3. It does NOT go below $30K. Dropping to $29,900 would save only $5,100 but would signal insecurity at this price point. Dealers who can afford a $34,900 setup fee are not price-shopping at the $1,000 level. Going to $29,900 tells them the price was inflated to begin with.

**What about $39,900?**

Not recommended for first clients. The premium positioning is correct for the mature product stage, but for the first 3-5 clients, the priority is proof — getting real dealerships on the platform, generating performance data, and building case studies. $39,900 adds friction at exactly the moment when reducing friction is most valuable. Raise to $39,900 or $44,900 after 5 paid installations.

### What the Setup Fee Must Include — the "Kit"

The setup fee must feel like a complete package, not a line item. Every component needs a name and a stated value. When a buyer sees the setup fee, they should understand what they are buying:

| Component | What It Is | Stated Value |
|---|---|---|
| Full Platform Installation | Custom installation of Reflex at the rooftop — not a software login, a configured system | Included |
| Sales Structure Build | 30-60 custom scenarios mapped to the dealership's actual objection profile and sales process | $8,500 value |
| 2 VR Headsets | Physical hardware, configured and branded — ready to use on day of installation | $2,800 value |
| Onboarding & Training Sprint | On-site or virtual 2-day onboarding with Sales Manager and GM — defines success metrics, trains the team on the system | $3,500 value |
| 90-Day Success Coach | Dedicated Reflex coach assigned to the dealership for the first 90 days — weekly check-in, scenario adjustments, performance review | $4,500 value |
| Performance Baseline Report | Before-installation audit of closing rate, ramp time, and rep-level performance — used as Day 0 benchmark | Included |

**Total stated value:** over $19,000 in bundled components. The setup fee of $34,900 buys a full system implementation, not a software license.

This framing is critical. Dealers who have been burned by "software that nobody uses" will respond to the physical hardware, the custom build, and the dedicated coach. These components signal that Reflex is doing the heavy lifting, not handing over a login and wishing them luck.

## SECTION 2: MONTHLY VALIDATION — $3,500/MONTH IS CORRECT

### What $3,500/Month Includes

Monthly is not just "maintenance." Frame it as ongoing service infrastructure:

- AI consumption costs (the AI runs millions of simulation interactions — this is real usage cost)
- Real-time vehicle inventory data sync (scenarios stay current with the dealership's actual inventory and pricing)
- Scenario library updates (new objection scenarios added monthly as the market changes — EV objections, rate environment changes, competitor cross-shop scenarios)
- Platform updates and feature releases
- Monthly performance report: rep-level data, closing rate tracking, scenario completion by objection type
- Ongoing 20-minute monthly strategy call with dedicated success coach

**The framing:** $3,500/month is not a maintenance fee. It is an operational subscription that keeps the training environment current with the dealership's real selling conditions.

### The Annual Math (Year 1 vs Year 2)

| | Year 1 | Year 2+ |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | $34,900 | $0 |
| Monthly (12 months) | $42,000 | $42,000 |
| **Total** | **$76,900** | **$42,000** |

**Year 1 cost: $76,900** — this is the number in the sales conversation.
**Year 2+ cost: $42,000/year** — this is the argument for long-term value. Once the setup is paid, the annual cost drops 45%. Frame this in the close: "Year 1 is your largest investment. Every year after that, you're getting the same system for $42K."

### Is $3,500/Month Defensible vs. Competitive Options?

Yes. The comparison set:

- **Joe Verde/JVTN:** $4,500-$9,000/year per dealership for online modules — passive video content, zero simulation. Reflex charges $42,000/year for active AI simulation.
- **NCM Associates training:** $5,000-$20,000 per event for in-person workshops — one-time knowledge transfer, no ongoing practice. Zero hardware.
- **In-house sales trainer:** $65,000-$95,000/year in salary + benefits + travel — one person, one location, inconsistent.
- **Sales Manager "rescue" cost:** If a Sales Manager spends 3 hours/day rescuing deals instead of coaching, at their fully-loaded cost of $120,000-$160,000/year, that is $45,000-$60,000/year in manager time burned on individual deals rather than team development.

The $42,000/year subscription competes directly with the cost of one in-house trainer — and delivers 24/7 availability, consistent quality, and rep-level performance data that no human trainer can produce.

## SECTION 3: FIRST-CLIENT OFFER — FOR THE FIRST 3-5 PAID DEALERSHIPS

The first-client offer exists for one reason: to remove the hesitation that comes from being an early adopter. It does NOT reduce the monthly. It does NOT give away the core product. It reduces friction on the upfront capital decision while protecting recurring revenue.

### The First Adopter Package

**Name:** "Founding Partner Program" — 5 slots available

**What changes:**
- Setup fee: **$24,900** (reduced from $34,900 — saves the dealer $10,000)
- Monthly: **$3,500/month** — unchanged
- Contract: 12-month minimum — unchanged

**What they get in addition to the standard package:**
- 4 VR Headsets instead of 2 (adds $2,800 hardware value — good PR when the headsets go on the floor)
- Named as a "Founding Partner" with recognition in Reflex's case study library
- First access to all new scenario modules before general release (6-month exclusivity window)
- Direct input into the product roadmap (2 scenario requests per quarter built custom for their store)

**The performance guarantee:**
"If your team's closing rate does not improve measurably within 90 days of going live, we will extend your subscription for 3 additional months at no charge and rebuild your scenario library — or refund your first month's payment. Your choice."

This guarantee is deliberately specific. "Improve measurably" means: an improvement in closing rate that both parties agree to define in writing at Day 0. This prevents vague disputes while giving the buyer the risk reversal they need to say yes.

### Why This Works

1. The $10,000 setup fee reduction is real and meaningful at this ticket size — it converts a $76,900 Year 1 to a $66,900 Year 1. That is a number a GM can often approve without taking it to the DP.
2. The extra headsets are physical, visible evidence of commitment. When the GM shows the DP the headsets, the purchase feels real and tangible — not a software expense.
3. The founding partner status creates urgency without being fake: only 5 slots exist because only 5 installations can be supported during the build phase without compromising quality.
4. The performance guarantee eliminates the last objection: "How do I know it works?" The answer is now: "You'll know in 90 days, and if it doesn't, you have options." This converts the risk from permanent to time-bounded.

### What the First-Client Offer Does NOT Do

- Does not reduce the monthly. The $3,500/month is the business model. Every dollar of discount should come from the one-time setup fee, never from the recurring.
- Does not extend to dealer groups at the group level. A dealer group can get the Founding Partner deal on their first rooftop, but subsequent rooftops price according to the dealer group structure below.
- Does not include more than 5 slots. When slots fill, the pricing reverts to $34,900 setup. This is not a gimmick — Reflex genuinely cannot support unlimited simultaneous first-client installations at high quality.

## SECTION 4: DEALER GROUP PRICING — PER-ROOFTOP ALWAYS

Hugo's position is correct: per-rooftop always. The structure below implements it in a way that does not feel like a penalty to the buyer.

### The Framing Problem Hugo Must Solve

If a dealer group hears "you pay full price for every rooftop," they feel penalized for scale. The psychological reframe is: the reduced setup fee on rooftop 2-5 is not a discount for being a group — it is an "efficiency bonus" because the sales structure build, onboarding, and baseline scenarios from rooftop 1 carry forward. Less build work on subsequent rooftops = reduced setup for those rooftops. The monthly never changes because the AI consumption, data sync, and coaching are per-rooftop operating costs.

### Group Structure

| Rooftop | Setup Fee | Monthly | Year 1 Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rooftop 1 | $34,900 | $3,500 | $76,900 |
| Rooftop 2 | $24,900 | $3,500 | $66,900 |
| Rooftop 3 | $19,900 | $3,500 | $61,900 |
| Rooftop 4-5 | $17,900 each | $3,500 | $59,900 each |
| Rooftop 6+ | $14,900 each | $3,500 | $56,900 each |

**Revenue per rooftop, Year 2+:** $42,000 flat regardless of group size. The setup reduction is on the one-time fee only.

### Presenting This to a Dealer Group

"Every rooftop has its own AI environment, its own inventory data feed, its own scenario library, and its own performance analytics — so monthly is always $3,500 per location. That's the operational cost of keeping each store's system running.

The setup fee decreases on each additional store because we carry over the sales structure and scenario architecture from your first installation. We do not rebuild from scratch — we adapt. That efficiency savings is passed directly to you."

This framing works because it is true. The AI build work, the custom scenario development, and the onboarding framework do carry forward. The monthly costs genuinely do not decrease because the per-rooftop operational costs are real.

### Enterprise Group Addendum (10+ Rooftops)

For groups with 10 or more rooftops:

- Setup fee: $14,900 per rooftop for all rooftops (flat rate at scale)
- Monthly: $3,000/month per rooftop (10% reduction — the only monthly reduction, justified by dedicated enterprise support model)
- Minimum: 2-year contract at this tier
- Add: Dedicated enterprise success manager (single point of contact for the group's training director)
- Add: Custom reporting dashboard aggregated across all rooftops (group-level analytics)

This tier exists for the AutoNation, Lithia, Asbury-level conversations. At 20 rooftops, the enterprise deal is $14,900 × 20 setup + $3,000 × 20 × 12 = $298,000 + $720,000 Year 1 = over $1M in first-year revenue from one group.

## SECTION 5: THE ROI ARGUMENT — BUILT FOR THE SALES CONVERSATION

This section contains the exact ROI math to use in a sales conversation or sales document. Numbers are conservative and verifiable.

### The Foundation: One Extra Deal Per Week

Every assumption below is based on one additional closed deal per week — the minimum plausible outcome from a training investment of this scale.

**Why one deal per week is conservative:**
- A 10-rep team that closes at 18% of floor traffic (industry average) is leaving 82% of traffic unsold
- A 2-3% improvement in closing rate on a floor with 50 weekly ups produces 1-1.5 additional deals per week
- This is not a 20% improvement claim — it is a marginal improvement argument that any skeptical GM can accept

### The Math

**Baseline assumption (conservative):**
- Additional deals per week: 1
- Weeks per year: 52
- Annual additional deals: 52
- Average front-end gross per deal: $3,500 (conservative — industry range is $3,000-$4,500 for franchised dealers)
- Annual additional gross profit from front-end: **$182,000**

**F&I uplift (often excluded from training ROI but real):**
If each additional deal includes F&I products at average $1,200 F&I gross:
- Annual F&I uplift: 52 × $1,200 = $62,400
- **Total annual gross uplift (front-end + F&I): $244,400**

**Conservative version for the sale (front-end only):**

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Annual additional gross profit | $182,000 |
| Year 1 Reflex investment | $76,900 |
| Net Year 1 return | **$105,100** |
| ROI (Year 1) | **136%** |
| Payback period | **~5 months** |

**Year 2 math (no setup fee):**

| | Amount |
|---|---|
| Annual additional gross profit | $182,000 |
| Year 2 Reflex investment | $42,000 |
| Net Year 2 return | **$140,000** |
| ROI (Year 2) | **333%** |

### The Visual Math to Use in a Sales Document or Sales Call

Frame it as a question, not a claim:

"Let me ask you a question. If your team closed one additional deal per week that they otherwise would have lost on an objection — how much is that worth to you in a year?

At your average gross of $3,500 per deal, 52 extra deals is $182,000 in additional gross profit. Reflex in Year 1 costs $76,900. That means if the system produces ONE extra deal per week — the minimum outcome we've seen, not the average — you net $105,000 on top of what you're paying.

Most stores see 2-3 additional deals per week after 90 days. But we don't need to sell you on the upside. We just need to agree that one deal per week is a credible minimum. If you agree with that, the math tells the rest of the story."

### The Ramp Time ROI (Secondary Argument — Use When Turnover Is the Hot Button)

At 67% annual turnover on a 10-rep team, 6-7 people leave per year. Each hire costs $10,000-$15,000 to replace (recruiting, onboarding, lost productivity during ramp).

If Reflex reduces new hire ramp time from 90 days to 45 days:
- Days recovered per hire: 45
- Productive days added per year (6 hires × 45 days): 270 additional productive rep-days per year
- At 0.3 deals/productive day (conservative): 81 additional deals from ramp time reduction alone
- At $3,500 gross per deal: **$283,500 in additional gross profit from ramp time reduction**

This argument is for the Dealer Principal who thinks about the structural cost of turnover, not just the floor manager who thinks about weekly closing rates.

## SECTION 6: OBJECTION PRE-HANDLING — SCRIPTS FOR THE $35K CONVERSATION

These are not theoretical responses. They are scripts to be used verbatim or near-verbatim in sales conversations.

---

### Objection 1: "That's a lot of upfront money."

**Context:** This objection is almost always about risk, not cash. The buyer has the money. They are asking: "What happens if this doesn't work?"

**Script:**

"I hear that. And I want to address what I think is the real question underneath it, which is: what happens if you pay this and it doesn't deliver?

Here's what we do: we define success in writing before installation starts. We sit down with your GM and your Sales Manager, and we agree on what 'working' means for your store — whether that's closing rate, gross per deal, or new hire ramp time. We write it down. That is your Day 0 benchmark.

At Day 90, we compare. If the number hasn't moved, you get three months free and a full rebuild of your scenario library. No argument, no small print.

The risk on the upfront is that you write a check and see nothing change. That's exactly what we've designed the guarantee to remove. The $34,900 buys a complete installation — hardware, custom scenarios, dedicated coach, and 90-day accountability. If we don't deliver on the metrics we agreed to, we work for free until we do."

**Why it works:** It names the real fear, reframes the upfront as a risk-managed investment, and routes directly to the guarantee.

---

### Objection 2: "We already have training."

**Context:** This is the most common objection. The buyer is not wrong — most dealerships do have some form of training. This objection is a comparison problem, not a rejection.

**Script:**

"That's actually the best situation to be in, because it means your team already knows what good sales behavior looks like. They've seen the playbook.

Here's the question I'd ask: can your rep watch a training video about handling a price objection on Thursday, then handle that objection correctly under pressure on Friday when a real buyer is pushing back? Research consistently shows that gap between knowing and doing — under real pressure — is where training breaks down. The forgetting curve alone means 80% of video training is gone within 48 hours.

Reflex doesn't replace what you already have. It fills the one gap nothing else fills: consequence-free repetition under realistic pressure. Your training teaches the knowledge. Reflex builds the reflex — the automatic, pressure-tested response that happens when a rep is in the moment and the customer pushes back. Those are different things.

What specific training are you using right now? I want to make sure I'm not describing a solution you've already tried."

**Why it works:** It respects the existing investment, names the specific gap (knowledge vs. execution under pressure), and ends with a diagnostic question that keeps the conversation open.

---

### Objection 3: "How do I know this works?"

**Context:** This is a buying signal. The buyer is not rejecting the product — they are asking for proof before committing. This is the correct objection to receive.

**Script:**

"That's the exact right question, and I'm going to give you an answer that's different from what every other vendor will give you.

I'm not going to show you a case study from a Walmart or an Amazon and ask you to believe it applies to your Honda store. Those are different businesses, different buyers, different pressure environments.

What I'm going to offer instead is this: let's measure it in your store, with your reps, against your own numbers. Before we install anything, we document your baseline — your current closing rate, your average gross per deal, your new hire ramp time. Whatever metric matters most to you.

At Day 90, we look at the same numbers. You don't have to believe anything I say today. The results come from your own operation.

And if the numbers don't move in 90 days? You get three free months and a full scenario rebuild. We're not asking you to take our word for it. We're asking you to let us prove it with your data."

**Why it works:** It avoids the trap of defending irrelevant case studies, shifts the proof from "our claims" to "your data," and deploys the guarantee as a risk eliminator rather than a sales tactic.

---

### Objection 4: "I need to think about it."

**Context:** This objection means one of three things: (a) the buyer is genuinely interested but hasn't finished evaluating, (b) there is an unspoken objection they haven't articulated, or (c) they are being polite about a soft no. The script below is designed to distinguish between the three.

**Script:**

"Of course — this is a real investment and it deserves real consideration. I want to make sure the time you spend thinking about it is productive.

Can I ask: is there a specific part of this you want to think through? Whether it's the upside case, the guarantee, how the implementation works, or something else — if I know what's on your mind, I can either give you what you need to make the decision or tell you honestly if there's something you should wait on.

The only thing I'll say is this: we have five Founding Partner slots at the $24,900 setup price, and we're having this conversation with a handful of dealerships this month. I'm not going to pressure you — if this is the right decision, it will still be right in two weeks. But the founding partner pricing and the extra headsets are for the first five dealerships that install, not indefinitely.

What's your timeline for deciding, and what would you need to see before you're ready?"

**Why it works:** It avoids the pressure-close trap by not pushing, respects the buyer's process, introduces the scarcity of the founding partner slots without manufacturing urgency, and ends with a diagnostic question that surfaces the real hesitation.

---

### Objection 5: "My reps won't use it."

**Context:** This is a legitimate operational concern, not a price objection. The buyer is worried about adoption.

**Script:**

"Every GM we talk to raises this — and they're thinking about the reps who are 40 years old and barely tolerate their CRM.

Here's what we've found: adoption breaks by generation, and your team probably has both. The 25-35 year old reps will fight each other for headset time in the first two weeks. That's not a guess — it's what happens when you put immersive technology on a sales floor. The older reps take 2-3 weeks longer.

But here's the structural answer: we build the adoption plan into the onboarding. The Sales Manager gets a monthly scenario assignment schedule. Reps complete two scenario sessions per week — 20 minutes each — and the performance data is visible to the Sales Manager. When a rep's scenario completion rate is visible on a dashboard alongside their closing rate, the motivation to use the system is built into the accountability structure.

We also have a 'competition mode' that shows rep rankings on a leaderboard inside the platform. The competitive environment on a car sales floor means this feature gets used without the manager having to push it.

Adoption is an implementation problem, not a technology problem. That's why the onboarding is included in the setup fee."

**Why it works:** It validates the concern, addresses the age/demographic reality honestly, and pivots to the structural solution (accountability data + competition features) rather than making unsupported adoption claims.

## SECTION 7: PRICING CONVERSATION SEQUENCE

The order in which pricing is presented matters as much as the number itself. Never open with the price.

### Recommended Sales Conversation Sequence

**Step 1 — Establish the problem cost ($0 invested, maximum receptivity)**

Before any price is mentioned, establish the cost of the current situation. Use this question:

"Before I walk you through what Reflex costs, I want to get a number from you. Roughly how many deals per month do you think your team loses on objections — price objections, trade objections, 'let me think about it'? Even a ballpark."

The GM will give a number. It is almost always between 5 and 20 per month. Whatever they say, multiply it by $3,500 and reflect it back:

"So at 10 deals per month, that's $35,000 in gross profit per month your floor is leaving on the table — $420,000 a year. That's the problem we're solving. Keep that number in mind."

**Step 2 — Present the ROI case before the price**

Walk through the conservative ROI scenario (1 deal/week, $182,000/year gross upside) before the price is mentioned. The buyer now has a value anchor of $182,000/year.

**Step 3 — Introduce the investment**

"The investment in Reflex for your store is $34,900 to install — that covers everything, including two VR headsets — and $3,500 per month for the full service. Year 1, you're looking at about $77,000.

Against the $182,000 in additional gross profit from one extra deal per week, that's a 136% return in Year 1. Every year after that, there's no setup fee — just $42,000 per year."

**Step 4 — Deploy the guarantee proactively (before they ask)**

"Before you respond on the price — here's what happens if it doesn't deliver. [Deliver guarantee script.]"

**Step 5 — Introduce founding partner pricing if needed**

If the buyer hesitates on price, introduce the founding partner offer as an available option, not a desperation concession:

"There's also something available right now that's worth knowing about. We have five Founding Partner slots where the setup is $24,900 instead of $34,900, and you get four headsets instead of two. Those are for the first five dealerships that install while we're still building the initial case study base. If the timing works for you, that's available."

## SECTION 9: WHAT TO DO AFTER THE FIRST 5 CLIENTS

Once 5 Founding Partner installations are complete:

1. **Raise setup fee to $39,900.** The founding partner discount is gone. The case studies justify the increase.
2. **Document each installation's 90-day results.** One statistic from one real dealership (e.g., "Closing rate improved from 17% to 22% at a Ford dealership in Texas after 90 days") is worth more than 100 data points from PwC.
3. **Use case studies in ads.** The LinkedIn campaign architecture (already built in the positioning strategy document) is designed for case study content. Feed it real data as soon as it exists.
4. **Build a "Year 2 retention" conversation.** At Month 9, have the success coach initiate the Year 2 renewal conversation. Renewals should be automatic — the pain of pulling out the headsets and reverting to no practice environment is higher than $42,000.
