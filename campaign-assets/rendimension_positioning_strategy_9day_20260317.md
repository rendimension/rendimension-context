## 7. VISUAL DIRECTION

### HOOK 1 VISUALS — "The Empty Lot Problem"

**What the image should show:**
A split composition. Left side: an empty lot or construction site — raw, dusty, uninspiring. Right side: the same angle showing a photorealistic render of the completed development — alive with people, landscaping, evening lighting. The contrast should be stark and immediate.

**Emotional feeling:** The gap between potential and reality. The viewer should feel the frustration of having a vision trapped in their head and the relief of seeing it materialize.

**Colors and composition:**
- Left side: muted, desaturated, gray-brown tones (the "before")
- Right side: warm, inviting, rich colors with evening/golden-hour lighting (the "after")
- Clean diagonal or vertical split line
- Text overlay minimal — let the contrast do the work
- Small Rendimension logo in corner

**What makes it different from competitors:**
Competitors show ONLY the finished render (right side). By showing the empty lot FIRST, you anchor the viewer in the developer's reality — the uncomfortable "nothing" they're staring at today. The render becomes a solution, not decoration.

**AI Generation Prompt (Google Nano Banana):**
"Split image. Left side: an empty urban lot with construction fence, dirt, no buildings, overcast sky, desaturated colors. Right side: same angle, photorealistic completed luxury mixed-use development, evening lighting, warm tones, people walking, landscaping, retail at ground floor. Clean vertical split line dividing the two halves. Architectural visualization quality. 1080x1080."

---

### HOOK 2 VISUALS — "The $47K Mistake"

**What the image should show:**
A construction site with visible rework — partially demolished walls, exposed framing, workers standing idle, construction materials scattered. The image should feel expensive and wasteful. Overlay text: a large dollar figure ($47,000) with a brief caption.

ALTERNATIVE: A side-by-side showing a 3D render highlighting a design problem (circled in red) versus a photo of that same problem discovered during construction with scaffolding, demolition, and delay.

**Emotional feeling:** "This could happen to me." The viewer should feel the stomach-drop of an expensive mistake that was preventable.

**Colors and composition:**
- Dominant: construction yellow, concrete gray, warning-sign feel
- Accent: red circles or arrows highlighting the problem area
- Bold typography for the dollar figure — this is the scroll-stopper
- No golden-hour beauty. This is meant to feel urgent and real.
- Documentary style, not polished

**What makes it different from competitors:**
Every competitor shows the happy ending (beautiful render). This shows what happens when you SKIP visualization. It's consequence framing — a territory no archviz studio has ever used in advertising. The viewer sees risk, not art.

**AI Generation Prompt (Google Nano Banana):**
"Construction site interior showing costly rework: partially demolished interior wall, exposed framing and wiring, construction debris on floor, two workers in hard hats standing idle looking at the damage. Fluorescent construction lighting, realistic, documentary photography style. Overlaid large bold text: $47,000 in the upper portion. 1080x1080."

### HOOK 3 VISUALS — "Pre-Sold Before Groundbreaking"

**What the image should show:**
A developer or sales executive in a modern sales center/showroom, standing next to a large screen displaying a photorealistic 3D render of a residential development. In the foreground, a couple (buyers) are leaning forward, engaged, pointing at the screen. The body language says: "We want in." A "SOLD" or "RESERVED" tag visible on a unit board in the background.

ALTERNATIVE: An aerial render of a residential development with 60% of units overlaid with "SOLD" or "RESERVED" badges, and the tagline "Construction starts in 6 months."

**Emotional feeling:** Momentum. Success. The excitement of a project that's already generating revenue before the first shovel hits dirt. The viewer should feel envy — "my competitor is doing this."

**Colors and composition:**
- Clean, modern, professional — sales center aesthetic
- Warm but business-focused lighting (not golden-hour dreamscape)
- The 3D render on screen should be clearly visible — it's the hero element
- Human interaction is critical: people reacting TO the render
- Green or blue "SOLD" badges for the reservation board
- Professional but accessible — this is a business scene, not an art gallery

**What makes it different from competitors:**
Competitors show the RENDER as the product. This shows the render IN USE — being presented to buyers who are committing. It shows the business outcome (sales happening) not the artistic output (pretty picture). The developer scrolling sees themselves in the presenter's position.

## 12. WHAT NOT TO DO (Common $9/Day Mistakes)

1. **Do NOT split test audiences.** At $9/day you cannot afford to split between two ad sets. One audience. One ad set. Three creative variants inside it.

2. **Do NOT run traffic campaigns.** Traffic is cheap clicks that go nowhere. Lead Gen forms capture information on Facebook — no website visit needed, no landing page required.

3. **Do NOT use "broad" targeting.** At $9/day, broad targeting means Facebook shows your ad to the cheapest users, not the most relevant ones. Use the interest stacks above.

4. **Do NOT change anything in the first 7 days.** The algorithm needs time to learn. Days 1-3: do not look at results. Days 4-7: monitor only. Day 8: evaluate.

5. **Do NOT use CBO (Campaign Budget Optimization).** With one ad set, CBO adds no value and can cause budget distribution issues. Set budget at the ad set level.

6. **Do NOT boost posts.** Boosting is a different ad type with worse targeting and worse optimization. Build the campaign in Ads Manager.

7. **Do NOT use video as your first format.** At $9/day, single image gives Meta more signal faster because the decision happens immediately (click or scroll). Video requires watch time before a lead action. Test video after you have a winning static image.
